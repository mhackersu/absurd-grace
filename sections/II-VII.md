# Chapter 7: Paradox and the Power of the Individual

*“For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: but whosoever shall lose his life for my sake and the gospel's, the same shall save it.”*  
— *Mark 8:35, KJV*

## Introduction

The power of the individual has always been a sacred, paradoxical force. This chapter explores the tension between the collective and the individual, and how Kierkegaard's *Knight of Faith* and the teachings of Jesus Christ redefine the moral and spiritual power of standing alone. In an era where conformity is algorithmically rewarded and digital mobs dominate discourse, the individual guided by love and moral clarity becomes a subversive presence.

We will walk through key existential teachings, revisit Kierkegaard, and anchor them in Scripture. The crowd may be loud, but as Kierkegaard warned: *"The crowd is untruth."*

## The Knight of Faith: Kierkegaard's Radical Individualism

Søren Kierkegaard’s concept of the *Knight of Faith* presents one of the most profound understandings of individuality within Christian thought. The Knight of Faith is not defined by social norms, nor by collective pressures, but by a radical personal relationship with the divine. This individual, paradoxically, is both part of the community and yet set apart from it in their absolute commitment to God.

In *Fear and Trembling*, Kierkegaard uses the biblical story of Abraham’s sacrifice of Isaac as a representation of the Knight of Faith. Abraham’s willingness to sacrifice his son in obedience to God is an act of individual faith that transcends reason. It defies human understanding, yet in that very act of defiance, Abraham expresses the deepest form of commitment to divine will. This paradoxical obedience is the foundation of true individuality.

The Knight of Faith acts not for the sake of personal gain, but for the fulfillment of a higher purpose. His or her faith is not derived from the crowd or societal approval, but from a personal covenant with God. The Christian ideal of radical obedience and surrender to God’s will challenges both the logic of collective thought and the comfort of conformity.

### Kierkegaard and the Paradox of Faith

Kierkegaard’s existential philosophy is built upon the tension between the finite and the infinite, the temporal and the eternal. The paradox of the Christian faith is that it calls one to embrace the absurd—things that seem irrational or contradictory to human understanding. In *Philosophical Fragments*, Kierkegaard explores the paradox of Christianity, emphasizing that true faith is not a matter of human reason or logic. It is a leap, a step into the unknown, trusting that the absurd will reveal the deepest truths about our relationship with God and the world.

This paradoxical stance is crucial to understanding Christian individuality. In the eyes of the world, the Christian life might seem strange, contradictory, and even foolish. But for the believer, it is the ultimate act of personal freedom. As Jesus stated in the Gospels, *“Whosoever shall lose his life for my sake shall find it.”* (Matthew 16:25, KJV). The paradox is that in yielding to God, the individual gains a new, truer form of life. This is the power of Christian individuality—it is the power to stand alone in faith, even in the face of societal rejection, knowing that the ultimate approval comes from God alone.

## The Crowd Is Untruth: The Collective vs. the Individual

In our modern world, the idea of the collective often overshadows the individual. Social media platforms, algorithm-driven content, and the push for conformity often make it seem that one must align with the group or risk being ostracized. However, Kierkegaard’s assertion that *“the crowd is untruth”* speaks directly to this modern phenomenon. The crowd, with its herd mentality and peer pressure, does not offer truth—it distorts it.

In the age of digital communication, we are constantly bombarded by the opinions and actions of others. Yet, the Christian ethic does not require conformity to the crowd, nor does it equate the number of followers with the presence of truth. Jesus’ teachings themselves often defied the conventional wisdom of the day. He called individuals to follow Him, not as part of a large, impersonal collective, but as unique, beloved persons with intrinsic value and purpose. This divine call is personal, not statistical.

In contrast to the conformity of the crowd, Jesus’ words emphasize a radical personal relationship with God. The Christian life is not about aligning oneself with the crowd’s preferences or opinions; it is about aligning oneself with the truth of God’s will. Jesus did not seek popularity or consensus; He sought the hearts of individuals, calling them to a deeper, more personal relationship with the divine.

## The Power of the Individual in Christ

True power, in the Christian sense, lies in the individual’s capacity to choose love, truth, and faith, even when it goes against the grain of societal norms. The early Christians understood this well. They were often persecuted for their refusal to conform to Roman practices, but they remained steadfast in their faith. The Apostle Paul, in his letters, often emphasizes the importance of individual moral agency, even in the face of external pressures.

*“For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh: (For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds;) Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ.”*  
— *2 Corinthians 10:3-5, KJV*

Paul’s words remind us that the power of the individual lies not in external strength or influence, but in the ability to align one’s thoughts and actions with the will of God. In this way, the individual becomes a warrior of truth and love, resisting the forces of evil and deception in the world.

### The Knight of Faith in the Modern World

The modern world presents unique challenges to the individual striving for faith and moral clarity. With the rise of digital culture and algorithm-driven behavior, it can feel impossible to stand alone. Yet, the call of the Knight of Faith remains as relevant today as it was in Kierkegaard’s time. In a world dominated by noise, distraction, and conformity, the individual who chooses to live according to the logic of love stands as a beacon of truth.

In practical terms, this means resisting the pressure to conform to digital trends, social expectations, and the logic of the masses. It means cultivating the inner strength to live by one’s convictions, even when those convictions go against popular opinion. It means embracing the paradox that, in losing oneself for the sake of God, one finds true freedom and purpose.

## Conclusion

The paradox of Christian faith is that by yielding to God, we gain ourselves. By losing our lives for the sake of love, we become fully alive. This chapter invites each reader to enter into that paradox—not to be consumed by the crowd, but to be shaped by the Cross. The power of the individual is not found in fame, wealth, or influence, but in the quiet, steadfast commitment to truth and love. It is a power that transcends the collective and stands firm in the face of cultural pressure.

*“I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me.”*  
— *Galatians 2:20, KJV*
