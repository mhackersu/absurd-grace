### 📚 **Theological and Philosophical Sources**

1. **The Holy Bible (King James Version)**  
   - All Scripture references are cited from the KJV.
   - Used extensively to ground chapters in divine authority and moral truth.

2. **Søren Kierkegaard**  
   - Themes of individual moral responsibility, the “leap of faith,” and the “Knight of Faith.”  
   - Especially influential for content on paradox, existential ethics, and truth vs. crowd.

3. **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz**  
   - Concepts of theodicy, pre-established harmony, and metaphysical optimism.  
   - Invoked in discussions around divine logic and moral coherence.

4. **Charles H. Spurgeon**  
   - Preaching style, theological clarity, and emphasis on grace and repentance.  
   - Referenced for tone and content that bridge scholarly insight and heartfelt conviction.

5. **Immanuel Kant & Aristotle (comparative ethical context)**  
   - Moral imperative and virtue ethics serve as contrasts and historical context.  
   - Explored primarily in Part II when rethinking ethical frameworks.

7. **Robin Wall Kimmerer – _Braiding Sweetgrass_**
