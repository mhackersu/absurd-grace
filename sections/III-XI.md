# Chapter 11: **Neighbor.exe: Rebooting the Greatest Commandment**  
*Subtitle: What loving your neighbor looks like when your neighbor is a digital avatar.*

In the digital age, the definition of “neighbor” has expanded beyond physical proximity and into the realm of the virtual. The people we interact with aren’t just those we see in person—they are also avatars in online communities, faces in virtual meetings, and usernames attached to images on our social feeds. What does it mean to love these neighbors? Can the commandment to “love thy neighbor as thyself” still apply in a world where we often relate to others through screens and algorithms?

This chapter explores the challenges and opportunities of loving our digital neighbors, considering how we can apply the timeless teachings of Scripture to our virtual relationships. From social media interactions to virtual gaming spaces, the concept of "neighbor" in the digital era presents new ethical dilemmas and invitations for deeper, more meaningful connection.



## **The Digitalization of Neighbor**

The term "neighbor" in the ancient world was primarily rooted in physical geography. It referred to those who lived nearby, in the same community or neighborhood, sharing common space and often common concerns. But in the digital world, the lines between proximity and community blur. Your neighbor may live in another time zone, be part of a completely different culture, or exist in an entirely different realm of reality, such as a virtual space in a multiplayer video game.

This shift has profound implications for the Christian understanding of the commandment to love one's neighbor. In the Gospels, when asked to define who a neighbor is, Jesus tells the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37). The Samaritan, a foreigner and social outcast, becomes the model of compassion—not because of his proximity to the injured man, but because of his willingness to show mercy, regardless of societal boundaries.

Today, the digital world is our new "neighbor." Whether we’re posting a comment on a stranger’s status, offering advice in an online forum, or simply clicking "like" on a social media post, we’re interacting with avatars, profiles, and digital personas—people who may be worlds away from us but who still deserve our compassion, respect, and love.



## **The New Commandment: Online Compassion**

The commandment to love your neighbor becomes more complex when that neighbor is a digital avatar. How do we express genuine care and love in a digital space, especially when it’s so easy to reduce others to profiles or posts? The digital medium often makes it easier to dehumanize others; avatars don’t have physical bodies, and usernames often don’t carry the same weight as real-life interactions.

Yet, Jesus’ call to love is not contingent on proximity or physical interaction. The early Christians were scattered, often living in communities far removed from each other. Yet, the letters of the Apostles, such as those written by Paul, were filled with exhortations to love one another deeply, even when distance separated them.

In the digital age, this love is more important than ever. We must strive to see the humanity behind the avatars, behind the screen names, behind the tweets and TikToks. As Immanuel Kant proposed, we are called to treat others as ends in themselves, never merely as means to an end. In the world of social media, this means resisting the urge to reduce others to their profiles or online personas. We are called to engage with the deeper, often hidden humanity of our digital neighbors.



## **The Temptations of Disconnection**

While digital spaces present new opportunities for connection, they also introduce new temptations. Social media, for example, is designed to engage and entertain, often at the expense of meaningful interaction. It’s easy to click "like" or scroll past someone’s post without thinking twice, but this behavior reinforces a culture of shallow engagement.

Moreover, the anonymity of online interactions can embolden us to say things we would never say face-to-face. In the relative safety of a screen, we can be unkind, divisive, or dismissive without facing immediate consequences. This makes it all the more difficult to follow Christ's command to love our neighbors when we’re engaging in spaces where unkindness is often the norm.

As Søren Kierkegaard warned, the mass society tends to encourage the “crowd mentality”—a mode of being where individual responsibility and ethical engagement are often drowned out by the noise of the masses. In the digital world, this is amplified. How many times have we witnessed online mobs attacking someone over a disagreement, the mob mentality taking over as people forget that there are real, complex human beings behind the avatars?

The challenge is clear: how do we maintain authentic, compassionate love in a space that often encourages the opposite?



## **Loving Your Neighbor in the Digital Era**

So, what does it look like to love your digital neighbor? How can we love others through our screens and avatars in a way that reflects Christ’s sacrificial love? It begins with the same foundation that Christ set in the Gospels: love is an active choice, not just a feeling. It’s choosing to see others with dignity, choosing to listen even when it’s easier to scroll, choosing to respond with kindness even when it’s difficult.

Here are some practical ways we can love our digital neighbors:

- **Engage with empathy**: Whether commenting on a post or participating in a discussion, we can choose to listen with empathy. We can ask ourselves: How would I respond if this person were in front of me? Would I offer them grace and compassion, or would I be dismissive?

- **Reject dehumanization**: It’s easy to reduce people to their digital profiles. But each avatar represents a real person, with their own experiences, struggles, and dreams. We must resist the urge to treat people as objects or entertainment. Instead, we are called to see them as image-bearers of God, deserving of love and respect.

- **Extend grace in disagreement**: Online platforms are filled with opportunities for disagreement. Yet, even in conflict, we are called to love. This doesn’t mean we must always agree with others, but it does mean we should approach disagreements with humility, listening more than speaking, and seeking common ground rather than winning the argument.

- **Be a witness to peace**: As we navigate the noisy digital world, we can be witnesses to the peace of Christ. By refusing to engage in online fights, by choosing compassion over contempt, and by fostering an environment of respect, we can become beacons of love in a world that often feels divided and hostile.



## **A New Kind of Neighbor**

In a digital world, loving our neighbor is not bound by geography. Our neighbors are those we interact with across social media, those we game with in virtual environments, and even those we communicate with through email or text. The commandment to love has taken on new forms, but its essence remains the same. 

In the end, loving your digital neighbor is about seeing beyond the screen. It’s about rejecting the dehumanizing tendencies of digital spaces and embracing the humanity of those on the other side of the avatar. It’s about showing kindness, grace, and respect, not just to those who are physically near us, but to everyone we encounter, online and offline.

The next time you interact with someone online—whether it’s a comment on a post or a private message—ask yourself: How can I show love to this person? In a world where avatars are everywhere, the call to love remains the same. Let us love as Christ loved us—deeply, sacrificially, and with a heart full of grace.



## **Conclusion: The Digital Kingdom of God**

Loving our neighbors in the digital age is not just a matter of being kind or polite online; it’s about recognizing the sacred in the digital spaces we occupy. Just as Jesus reached out to the Samaritan woman at the well, so too are we called to reach out to our digital neighbors, offering them grace and love regardless of their background or circumstances.

In this new digital age, the Kingdom of God is expanding into spaces we might never have imagined. And the commandment to love our neighbor is no longer limited to the person next door—it extends across time zones, continents, and avatars. Loving our neighbor in the digital era means embracing the challenge of seeing Christ in every person we encounter online, and in doing so, expanding the reach of God’s love in ways we never thought possible.
