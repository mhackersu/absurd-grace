# 📱✨ Faith, Fame, and the Gospel According to Wi-Fi: A Satirical Pilgrimage Through the Digital Desert

> “In the beginning was the Word... and the Word went viral.”

Welcome to the collision of Sunday morning sanctity and algorithmic madness—a place where prophets wear ring lights, communion is attempted via touchscreen, and grace competes with branding in a brutal SEO deathmatch.

This is not your grandmother’s theology. This is **faith in the age of influencers**, and it’s complicated, commodified, and at times, unintentionally hilarious.

My forthcoming book dives headlong into the deep end of that sacred mess, with four sprawling, interconnected parts. Below is your fast-pass tour: a theological, satirical travelogue that asks what becomes of Jesus when His followers start chasing the algorithm harder than the gospel.

---

## Part I: *Faith in the Digital Spotlight*

**Alternate title:** *Jesus Got Meme’d and All I Got Was This Lousy Deconstruction Podcast.*

We begin where most religious experiences now do: online. What happens when Michael Jordan, Michael Jackson, and your megachurch pastor start blurring into the same digital celebrity archetype? We get *The Gospel According to Big Mike*, where the divine image is filtered, hashtagged, and monetized. Kierkegaard weeps in Danish.

We then explore how Jesus has become meme material, how prophets now drop episodes instead of oracles, and how ministers moonlight as brand ambassadors. And all of it leaves us wondering: **Is that the Holy Spirit, or just a very effective content strategy?**

---

## Part II: *Faith in the Age of Influencers*

**Working subtitle:** *Anointing Oil, Now with Prime Shipping.*

Here, the sacred gets sponsored. Christian practices are commodified faster than you can say “link in bio.” Anointing oils, miracle waters, and theological takes are now available in bulk—with reviews.

We also ask serious questions, like:  
- Can you truly receive the Eucharist through an iPad?  
- Is Jesus cool, or just eternally cringe?  
- And what does Kant think about TikTok testimonies?

Answer: Not much, but he'd probably ghost you mid-lecture.

This section leans into uncomfortable truths—like the fact that Jesus probably wouldn’t go viral, and certainly wouldn’t be invited on a panel with today’s spiritual thought leaders (unless He brought snacks).

---

## Part III: *Reclaiming Real Life in a Virtual World*

**Spoiler alert:** The algorithm will not save you.

If Jesus wasn’t cool, then neither is His love. It’s awkward, inconvenient, and wildly unbrandable. This section is a theological mic-drop on influencer culture, marketing ministries, and the temptation to replace presence with performance.

We explore:
- The existential discipline of putting down your phone.
- The divine disruption of loving your actual neighbors.
- And the deeply offensive comedy of the cross in an age of curated perfection.

Bonhoeffer, Kierkegaard, and Eugene Peterson all make cameos—none of whom ever trended on Twitter, and that’s kind of the point.

---

## Part IV: *The Sacred Struggle for Authentic Ministry*

**AKA:** *Grace Is Not a Brand Strategy (But Try Telling That to the PR Team).*

Here we slow down—literally. Sabbath becomes a countercultural act, a full-stop in a world addicted to productivity and public image. It’s a call to reject the dopamine-drip of visibility in favor of something older, quieter, and infinitely more faithful.

This part challenges the foundational assumptions of modern digital ministry:
- Should we really be calling the internet a mission field?
- What happens when grace is sold like software?
- And is it possible—just maybe—that the most faithful lives are the ones we never hear about?

Here we confront the radical idea that *love without applause is actually the most revolutionary kind of love.*

---

## TL;DR:

This book is a satirical theological exposé on faith in a hyperconnected world. It’s equal parts Kierkegaardian lament, Flannery O’Connor absurdity, and righteous internet critique. It’s about resisting the branding of the sacred, reclaiming the mess of real community, and daring to believe that **the gospel still matters, even if it never trends again.**

So if you’re tired of pixelated spirituality and want something that tastes more like communion than content—stick around. The absurd beauty of unmarketable grace is still very much alive.
