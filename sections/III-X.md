# Chapter 10: **Learning to Love (Even When You’d Rather Scroll)**  
*Subtitle: On presence, attention, and the rebellion of care.*

In an age of endless digital noise, the act of loving seems to be slipping through the cracks of our over-scheduled, overstimulated lives. We are bombarded with notifications, swiping through feeds, endlessly scrolling through lives that seem more exciting than our own. We’ve become conditioned to be constantly distracted, our attention divided into a thousand places at once. But love, true love, requires attention—intentional, focused, and present. 

As the world becomes more fragmented and disconnected, the very act of being present and caring for those around us is becoming an act of rebellion. In this chapter, we will explore the tension between the demands of our digital culture and the call to love in the here and now.



## **The Battle for Attention**

We live in a world where attention is the ultimate currency. Social media platforms, news outlets, advertisers—they all compete for our attention, using every tool at their disposal to keep us engaged, to keep us scrolling. Every time we check our phones, we are bombarded by notifications, each one designed to pull us in deeper, to keep us hooked.

This digital tug-of-war for our attention is not neutral. It shapes the way we interact with the world, the way we perceive ourselves and others. We’ve become accustomed to fleeting interactions, scrolling past people’s lives in a matter of seconds, never truly seeing or hearing them. The more we scroll, the less we truly engage. The more we “like” and “comment,” the less we genuinely care. 

As a result, love has become a casualty of our distracted world. How can we love when our attention is spread so thin? How can we truly be present for the people in our lives when we are constantly divided by the demands of our devices? The call to love requires a radical shift in how we view attention—it requires us to reclaim our focus, to choose presence over distraction.



## **The Rebellion of Presence**

The act of loving someone is inherently an act of presence. Love requires us to stop and look, to pay attention and engage deeply. When we love, we make ourselves available, both physically and emotionally. We show up for people, listen to their struggles, laugh with them in moments of joy, and mourn with them in their grief.

But in a world that thrives on speed, instant gratification, and constant stimulation, presence is becoming a revolutionary act. It’s easy to be there physically, but to truly be present—to give someone our undivided attention—is a rare gift. And it’s a gift that requires intentionality.

In a culture where we are encouraged to do more, be more, and consume more, the simple act of pausing and focusing on the person in front of us feels countercultural. It’s rebellious to slow down. It’s rebellious to turn off the notifications, to put the phone down, and to give someone your full, undistracted attention. But it is precisely this act of rebellion that brings love into the world.

As Søren Kierkegaard once wrote, *“Life can only be understood backwards; but it must be lived forwards.”* In a world obsessed with the future and the next notification, presence calls us to live in the now, to see the person in front of us—not as a potential like or share—but as a human being worthy of our full attention.



## **Love in the Age of Digital Distraction**

We know the statistics: the average person spends hours a day on their phone, checking notifications, scrolling through social media, and watching videos. It’s easy to feel overwhelmed by the constant barrage of information. But in the midst of all this noise, there is an invitation—to pause and choose love. 

Jesus, in His ministry, demonstrated the power of focused attention. He didn’t rush from one person to the next, jumping from task to task. Instead, He took the time to look people in the eye, to listen to their stories, and to be fully present with them. Whether it was a woman at a well, a man in a tree, or a child brought to Him for a blessing, Jesus gave them His undivided attention. He knew that love isn’t just about doing—it’s about being. 

In Mark 10:14 (KJV), we read that when the disciples tried to shoo children away, Jesus rebuked them, saying, *“Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.”* In this moment, Jesus showed us that true love doesn’t rush past the inconvenient or the unimportant—it stops, it listens, it engages. He made room for the ones who were easy to overlook.

If we are to love like Jesus, we must resist the temptation to live in a perpetual state of distraction. We must resist the urge to scroll through our lives, skimming over the moments that matter most. Instead, we must lean in, choose presence, and invest our attention in the people and the moments that truly matter.



## **The Cost of Not Paying Attention**

In a world where distractions are constant, paying attention can feel like a luxury. But the cost of failing to pay attention is high. When we fail to focus on the people around us, we miss out on the deep connections that form the foundation of love. We miss the small moments that make a relationship meaningful: the shared laugh, the quiet moment of understanding, the hand held in comfort.

In addition, our failure to pay attention can harm our spiritual lives. God calls us to love our neighbors, to bear one another’s burdens, and to walk alongside each other in times of joy and sorrow. But how can we do this if we are constantly distracted? How can we truly love our neighbors if we can’t even look up from our phones long enough to see them?

In Matthew 25:40 (KJV), Jesus says, *“Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.”* Every act of attention, every moment of presence, is an opportunity to love Christ through loving others. When we fail to give our attention, we miss the chance to serve Christ in the most profound way.



## **The Practice of Attention as an Act of Love**

So, how do we reclaim attention in a world that is constantly pulling us in a thousand directions? It begins with recognizing the value of presence and making the conscious decision to give our full attention to those around us. It requires us to be intentional, to make space for others in our busy lives, and to resist the pull of the digital world that tells us we must be constantly connected, constantly entertained, and constantly moving.

The practice of attention is not about perfection—it’s about consistency. It’s about choosing, moment by moment, to be present. It’s about looking up from our screens and truly seeing the people around us. It’s about listening—not just hearing, but listening deeply. It’s about sitting with someone in their joy and in their pain, without rushing to the next task.

In the end, love is not about what we do—it’s about who we are willing to be. It’s about choosing presence over distraction, care over indifference, and connection over isolation. As we learn to love in the digital age, we are called to practice the rebellion of attention—to refuse the easy path of scrolling, and to choose the harder, more rewarding path of loving with our full hearts and undivided focus.



## **Conclusion: Reclaiming Presence in a Distracted World**

In a world filled with noise, the call to love can often feel drowned out. But the antidote to distraction is not more noise, but more presence. The antidote to scrolling is more focus. And the antidote to isolation is more connection. 

As we navigate this age of distraction, let us remember that love is not found in fleeting moments or viral trends—it is found in the deep, quiet, and intentional acts of care that require us to be present. Love is found when we choose to stop scrolling and start truly seeing the people in front of us.

Jesus taught us that the greatest commandment is to love God and love our neighbors as ourselves (Matthew 22:37-39). To do this, we must give the gift of our full attention. In a world of distractions, this is the rebellion that truly matters. And it is in this rebellion that love, true and deep, is found.
